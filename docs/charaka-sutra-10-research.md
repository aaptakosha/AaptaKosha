## Thematic map

1. **10.1–10.6 — What makes medicine effective**
   - Four-pada / sixteen-quality medicine.
   - Maitreya's objection from apparently similar outcomes.
   - Atreya's distinction between treatable and untreatable disease.
   - Examination before action and the physician's role.
   - Therapeutic examples: depletion, nourishment, thermal correction, dhātu restoration/reduction, and root-opposite treatment.

2. **10.7–10.10 — Physician must distinguish sādhya and asādhya**
   - Acting at the proper time.
   - Risks of treating asādhya disease.
   - Four broad disease categories: sukhasādhya, kṛcchrasādhya, yāpya, and anupakrama/asādhya.
   - Three grades of treatable disease.

3. **10.11–10.17 — Features supporting ease/difficulty of treatment**
   - Hetu, pūrvarūpa and rūpa.
   - Doṣa/dūṣya, prakṛti, kāla and deśa considerations.
   - Single-path vs multiple-path disease.
   - Acute/recent vs persistent disease.
   - Yāpya disease and limited comfort.
   - Difficult disease characteristics.

4. **10.18–10.22 — Severe/asādhya disease and clinical examination**
   - Deep, multi-dhātu, marma/sandhi involvement.
   - Dvi-doṣaja/tridoṣaja patterns.
   - Transgression of the therapeutic pathway and multi-mārga spread.
   - Indriya loss, debility, sāriṣṭa signs.
   - Examination before intervention in sādhya disease.
   - Correct classification prevents Maitreya-like mistaken reasoning.

5. **10.23–10.24 — Chapter summary**
   - Medicine, four-pada qualities, cause/source of bheṣaja.
   - Atreya/Maitreya views.
   - Fourfold disease classification and individual disease signs.
   - Successful physician action depends on the four-pada framework.
