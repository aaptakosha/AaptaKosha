# Kriya Sharir — Chapter 1: Sharir (शरीर)

> **NCISM Paper I · Part A · Topic 1 · Term I**  
> This chapter covers the official NCISM learning scope: definition and synonyms of **Kriya, Sharira and Shaarira**; **Sharira Dosha and Manasa Dosha**; and the mutual relationship of **Triguna, Panchamahabhuta and Tridosha**.

## 1. Chapter Overview

**Kriya Sharir** is the study of normal functions and functional principles of the living body from both Ayurvedic and modern physiological perspectives.

The first topic, **Sharir**, gives the conceptual foundation for the whole subject. Before studying individual Doshas, Dhatus, Agni, Mala, Indriya, Manas and organ-system physiology, a student should understand:

- what is meant by **Sharira**;
- what **Kriya** means;
- what is meant by **Shaarira**;
- the difference between **Sharira Dosha** and **Manasa Dosha**;
- how **Panchamahabhuta**, **Tridosha** and **Triguna** are related;
- and why these concepts are frameworks for understanding normal function rather than one-to-one equivalents of modern anatomical structures.

### Why this chapter matters

This topic is short in the syllabus but conceptually important. It becomes the base for later chapters on Tridosha, Prakriti, Dhatu, Mala, Manas and applied physiology.

---

## 2. Learning Objectives

After studying this chapter, you should be able to:

1. Define **Kriya, Sharira and Shaarira**.
2. Explain important synonyms and etymological ideas related to Sharira.
3. Distinguish **Sharira** from **Shaarira**.
4. Enumerate **Sharira Dosha** and **Manasa Dosha**.
5. Explain the functional significance of Vata, Pitta and Kapha at a foundational level.
6. Explain the basic meaning of **Sattva, Rajas and Tamas**.
7. Describe the five Mahabhutas.
8. Explain the conceptual relationship among Panchamahabhuta, Tridosha and Triguna.
9. Understand that Ayurvedic correlations with modern physiology are conceptual and should not automatically be treated as identical scientific entities.

# 4. What is Kriya?

## Definition

**Kriya (क्रिया)** broadly means **activity, action, operation or function**.

For Kriya Sharir, the important idea is:

> **Kriya = the normal functional activity of the living body.**

Examples of physiological functions include:

- respiration;
- digestion;
- circulation;
- excretion;
- movement;
- sensory perception;
- maintenance of internal balance;
- sleep and wakefulness;
- reproductive functions.

### Simple way to remember

**Rachana asks:** “What is the structure?”  
**Kriya asks:** “How does it function?”

So:

**Rachana Sharir → structure**  
**Kriya Sharir → function**

This distinction is useful throughout BAMS.

# 5. What is Sharira?

## Important classical etymological idea

A commonly taught Ayurvedic derivation is:

**शीर्यते इति शरीरम् ।**

**Shīryate iti śarīram** — that which undergoes continuous deterioration/decay is termed Sharira.

The idea is not that the body is simply “destroyed”; rather, the body is a dynamic living system undergoing continuous change, turnover, growth, repair and ageing.

### Concept in simple words

Your body is never completely static.

- Cells are replaced.
- Proteins are synthesized and degraded.
- Tissues adapt.
- Energy is continuously produced and consumed.
- Growth occurs.
- Repair occurs.
- Ageing occurs.

Therefore, **Sharira is understood as a dynamic, changing living body**.

Different Ayurvedic texts and teaching traditions use several related terms.

---

# 7. Sharira as a Dynamic System

A useful mental model is:

**Nutrition → Transformation → Tissue maintenance → Function → Waste formation → Elimination → Renewal**

This continuous process explains why physiology is fundamentally about **dynamic equilibrium**, not a permanently fixed state.

### Modern physiology connection

The modern concept of **homeostasis** similarly describes the ability of a living organism to maintain relatively stable internal conditions despite continuous internal and external changes.

**Important:** Homeostasis is a modern physiological concept. It can be used as a teaching correlation with Ayurvedic ideas of functional balance, but it should not be declared identical to any single Ayurvedic concept.

---

# 8. What is Shaarira?

**Shaarira (शारीर)** means that which is **related to, concerning, or pertaining to Sharira**.

Thus:

- **Sharira** = the body.
- **Shaarira** = pertaining to the body / knowledge concerning the body.

### Why this matters

In academic terminology:

**Sharira** may refer to the body itself.

**Shaarira** may be used adjectivally for knowledge, principles or descriptions related to the body.

This distinction helps students understand the names of Ayurvedic disciplines.

---

# 9. Sharira vs Shaarira

| Feature | Sharira | Shaarira |
|---|---|---|
| Basic meaning | Body | Pertaining to the body |
| Nature | Entity/concept | Adjectival/disciplinary sense |
| Example | Human body | Sharira-related knowledge |
| Student cue | “What is the body?” | “What concerns the body?” |

### One-line memory

> **Sharira = body; Shaarira = pertaining to the body.**

# 10. Sharira Dosha

The three **Sharira Dosha** are:

1. **Vata**
2. **Pitta**
3. **Kapha**

They are described as major functional regulatory principles of the body.

## High-yield concept

**Dosha is not simply a physical substance.**

For introductory learning, understand Dosha as a functional regulatory framework used by Ayurveda to explain physiological processes, tendencies and pathological disturbance.

---

# 11. Three Sharira Dosha at a Glance

| Dosha | Dominant Mahabhuta association | Broad functional idea |
|---|---|---|
| Vata | Akasha + Vayu | Movement, transport, communication, propulsion |
| Pitta | Agni + Jala | Transformation, digestion, metabolism |
| Kapha | Jala + Prithvi | Stability, cohesion, lubrication, support |

### Important caution

These elemental combinations are a classical conceptual framework. They are **not chemical compositions** and should not be interpreted as literal percentages of physical elements in the human body.

# 12. Vata — Foundational Functional Idea

Vata is broadly associated with **movement and regulation of movement**.

At an introductory level, think of:

- movement;
- propulsion;
- transmission;
- circulation of impulses;
- respiratory movements;
- movement of materials;
- elimination.

Its five subdivisions will be studied in detail in the dedicated **Vata Dosha** chapter.

# 15. Manasa Dosha

The two traditionally described **Manasa Dosha** are:

1. **Rajas**
2. **Tamas**

### What about Sattva?

**Sattva** is described as a quality associated with clarity, balance, knowledge and harmony.

In introductory Ayurvedic teaching:

- **Sattva** → clarity and balanced functioning of mind.
- **Rajas** → activity, excitation, movement and passion.
- **Tamas** → inertia, heaviness, obscuration and inactivity.

Rajas and Tamas are classically treated as the principal **Manasa Dosha**, while Sattva is regarded as a desirable mental quality rather than a Dosha.

### 2. Rajas

### 3. Tamas

# 17. Sharira Dosha vs Manasa Dosha

| Feature | Sharira Dosha | Manasa Dosha |
|---|---|---|
| Number | 3 | 2 |
| Members | Vata, Pitta, Kapha | Rajas, Tamas |
| Primary domain | Sharira / bodily functions | Manas / mental functioning |
| Broad role | Regulation of bodily processes | Mental activity and disturbance |
| Study areas | Physiology, Dosha, Prakriti, disease | Manas, behaviour, mental health |
| Sattva | Not counted as Sharira Dosha | Important positive mental quality |

### Exam answer

**Sharira Dosha:** Vata, Pitta, Kapha.  
**Manasa Dosha:** Rajas, Tamas.

# 18. Panchamahabhuta

Ayurveda describes five great elements:

1. **Akasha (आकाश)** — space/ether
2. **Vayu (वायु)** — air/movement principle
3. **Agni/Tejas (अग्नि/तेजस्)** — transformation/fire principle
4. **Jala/Apa (जल/आप)** — fluidity/water principle
5. **Prithvi (पृथ्वी)** — solidity/earth principle

These are **fundamental conceptual categories** in Ayurvedic physiology and philosophy.

They should not be equated literally with the modern chemical elements of the periodic table.

---

# 19. Panchamahabhuta — Functional Understanding

| Mahabhuta | Core quality/idea | Useful physiological teaching analogy |
|---|---|---|
| Akasha | Space | Spaces/cavities allowing processes to occur |
| Vayu | Movement | Movement and dynamic activity |
| Agni | Transformation | Metabolic/transformation processes |
| Jala | Fluidity/cohesion | Fluid and cohesive properties |
| Prithvi | Solidity/stability | Structural and supportive properties |

**Important:** These are analogical learning aids, not claims of scientific identity.

---

# 20. Relationship: Panchamahabhuta → Tridosha

A classical association is:

**Akasha + Vayu → Vata**

**Agni + Jala → Pitta**

**Jala + Prithvi → Kapha**

### Visual memory map

**PANCHAMAHABHUTA**

→ Akasha + Vayu → **VATA**

→ Agni + Jala → **PITTA**

→ Jala + Prithvi → **KAPHA**

Notice that **Jala participates in both Pitta and Kapha**. This is an important exam point.

---

# 21. Why Do Doshas Arise from Mahabhutas?

The Mahabhutas represent broad qualitative principles.

When particular combinations of these qualities are considered in a living system, Ayurveda describes three major functional patterns:

- Vata — movement/dynamic regulation;
- Pitta — transformation;
- Kapha — stability/cohesion.

Thus the Mahabhuta framework provides a conceptual foundation for understanding the qualities and functions attributed to Tridosha.

---

# 22. Relationship: Triguna and Tridosha

This is one of the most commonly confused areas.

### Important principle

**Triguna and Tridosha are different classifications.**

**Triguna:**
- Sattva
- Rajas
- Tamas

**Tridosha:**
- Vata
- Pitta
- Kapha

They should **not** be memorized as fixed one-to-one pairs.

### Why?

Triguna primarily describes qualities related to **Manas and nature**, whereas Tridosha is used primarily to explain **Sharira-level functional regulation**.

They can influence and interact conceptually, but:

> **Sattva ≠ Vata**  
> **Rajas ≠ Pitta**  
> **Tamas ≠ Kapha**

These simplistic equations should be avoided.

---

# 23. Relationship: Triguna ↔ Panchamahabhuta ↔ Tridosha

A useful conceptual model is:

**Panchamahabhuta**  
↓  
Provides qualitative/functional elemental framework  
↓  
**Tridosha**  
↓  
Explains major bodily functional patterns

Alongside this:

**Triguna**  
↓  
Describes qualities influencing mental functioning and behaviour  
↓  
**Manasa Dosha**  
↓  
Rajas + Tamas are considered principal mental Doshas

### Key conclusion

These frameworks are **interrelated but not interchangeable**.

# 25. Difficult Concept Explained

## “Are Doshas physical substances?”

**No—not in the simple modern anatomical sense.**

They are better understood as Ayurvedic functional principles/frameworks that describe patterns of physiological activity and their disturbance.

### Analogy

Think of a modern dashboard:

- heart rate;
- blood pressure;
- temperature;
- respiratory rate.

The dashboard is not itself the heart, kidney or lung. It is a way of understanding functional state.

Similarly, do not search for a single anatomical “Vata organ” or “Pitta gland”.

---

# 26. Ayurveda–Modern Physiology Correlation

| Ayurvedic framework | Possible teaching correlation | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Vata | Movement, neural activity, transport and propulsion | Not identical to the nervous system or any single substance |
| Pitta | Transformation, digestion and metabolic processes | Not identical to enzymes or metabolism alone |
| Kapha | Stability, lubrication and structural support | Not identical to mucus, connective tissue or any single substance |
| Homeostatic balance | Functional equilibrium | Homeostasis is a modern physiological concept and not a complete synonym for Ayurvedic equilibrium |
| Panchamahabhuta | Qualitative/functional model | Not equivalent to modern chemical elements |
| Triguna | Mental qualities and behavioural tendencies | Not a modern neurochemical classification |

# 28. Common Confusions

### Confusion 1
**Rajas is Pitta.**

❌ Incorrect.

Rajas is a **Guna** and is associated with Manas. Pitta is a **Sharira Dosha**.

### Confusion 2
**Tamas is Kapha.**

❌ Incorrect.

There may be conceptual similarities in qualities, but they are different classifications.

### Confusion 3
**Panchamahabhuta means five chemical elements.**

❌ Incorrect.

They are Ayurvedic conceptual categories, not the modern periodic-table elements.

### Confusion 4
**Sharira and Shaarira are completely unrelated words.**

❌ Incorrect.

Shaarira refers to that which pertains to Sharira.

### Confusion 5
**Dosha means disease.**

❌ Incorrect.

Dosha is a functional Ayurvedic concept. When disturbed, Doshas can participate in pathogenesis.

# 37. Clinical Learning Connection

This chapter is foundational rather than disease-focused.

The clinically important idea is that Ayurveda interprets health as appropriate functioning and balance of regulatory factors.

In modern physiology, students will later learn:

- neural regulation;
- endocrine regulation;
- cardiovascular regulation;
- respiratory regulation;
- renal regulation;
- temperature regulation;
- acid-base balance;
- fluid and electrolyte balance.

The conceptual bridge is:

> **A living organism must continuously regulate itself despite continuous change.**

The two systems use different explanatory frameworks and terminology. They should be studied faithfully rather than forced into artificial equivalence.

# 39. Final Takeaway

The first lesson of Kriya Sharir is not memorizing isolated Sanskrit terms.

It is learning to see the body as a **dynamic, continuously functioning system**.

Ayurveda uses interconnected conceptual frameworks:

**Sharira → Tridosha → Panchamahabhuta**

and

**Manas → Triguna → Manasa Dosha**

Understanding these relationships makes later chapters on Vata, Pitta, Kapha, Prakriti, Dhatu, Mala and applied physiology much easier.
