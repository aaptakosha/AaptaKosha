# First Professional — Sanskrit Chapter 1: Varṇa Vicāra and Māheśvara Sūtrāṇi

**Audience:** BAMS First Professional learners  
**Subject area:** Samskritam evam Ayurveda Itihas

## Learning objectives

By the end of this topic, a learner should be able to:
1. Explain the terms varṇa, svara, vyañjana, and pratyāhāra.
2. Recognize the fourteen Māheśvara Sūtras and explain the role of their final markers (it/anubandha letters) at an introductory level.
3. Identify common places of articulation used in Sanskrit phonetics.
4. Distinguish voiced/unvoiced and unaspirated/aspirated consonants in the traditional teaching framework.
5. Apply these concepts to accurate reading and pronunciation of Sanskrit terminology in Ayurvedic texts.

## 1. Varṇa: meaning and role

**Varṇa (वर्ण)** is a basic speech sound/phonological unit represented in the writing system by a letter. In Sanskrit study, accurate recognition and pronunciation of varṇas supports recitation, sandhi, grammatical analysis, and correct reading of Ayurvedic source texts.

Two broad introductory groups are:
- **Svara (स्वर):** vowels, which can be pronounced without being dependent on a following consonant.
- **Vyañjana (व्यञ्जन):** consonants, whose pronunciation ordinarily involves a constriction or contact in the vocal tract and are taught together with a vowel in practical recitation.

Traditional grammatical terminology uses **अच् (ac)** for the vowel class and **हल् (hal)** for the consonant class. These are Pāṇinian pratyāhāras, not ordinary standalone alphabet names.

## 2. Māheśvara Sūtras

The Māheśvara Sūtras are fourteen sound sequences used in the Pāṇinian grammatical tradition to form compact sound-class designations (pratyāhāras). The final sound in each sequence functions as a technical marker (it/anubandha) in the grammatical system and is not treated like an ordinary member of the resulting sound class.

The standard sequence is:

1. अइउण्
2. ऋऌक्
3. एओङ्
4. ऐऔच्
5. हयवरट्
6. लण्
7. ञमङणनम्
8. झभञ्
9. घढधष्
10. जबगडदश्
11. खफछठथचटतव्
12. कपय्
13. शषसर्
14. हल्

**Study caution:** The marker consonants at the ends of these sequences have a technical function. Do not simply memorize each sequence as an ordinary list of alphabet letters; learn how pratyāhāras are formed in the relevant grammar lessons.

### Selected pratyāhāras

- **अच् (ac):** the vowel class.
- **हल् (hal):** the consonant class.

A pratyāhāra is a compact label formed according to Pāṇinian convention, using a sound at the beginning of a sequence and a technical marker. Detailed derivation should be taught with the prescribed grammar reference.

## 3. Introductory places of articulation (उच्चारणस्थान)

Traditional Sanskrit phonetics describes sounds by their place and manner of articulation. Common classroom groupings include:

| Group/sounds | Traditional place | Introductory description |
|---|---|---|
| क-वर्ग: क ख ग घ ङ | कण्ठ (kaṇṭha) | Guttural/throat region |
| च-वर्ग: च छ ज झ ञ | तालु (tālu) | Palatal region |
| ट-वर्ग: ट ठ ड ढ ण | मूर्धा (mūrdhā) | Retroflex/cerebral region |
| त-वर्ग: त थ द ध न | दन्त (danta) | Dental region |
| प-वर्ग: प फ ब भ म | ओष्ठ (oṣṭha) | Labial/lip region |
| ए, ऐ | कण्ठतालु (kaṇṭhatālu) | Throat + palate in the traditional classification |
| ओ, औ | कण्ठोष्ठ (kaṇṭhoṣṭha) | Throat + lips in the traditional classification |

Some teaching traditions describe additional articulation details, especially for vowels and semivowels. Use the exact classification prescribed by the adopted Sanskrit grammar text for examinations.

## 4. Voicing and aspiration

- **घोष (ghoṣa):** voiced sounds, produced with vocal-fold vibration.
- **अघोष (aghoṣa):** voiceless sounds, produced without the same vocal-fold vibration.
- **अल्पप्राण (alpaprāṇa):** unaspirated or comparatively low-expiration consonants in the traditional classification.
- **महाप्राण (mahāprāṇa):** aspirated or comparatively strong-expiration consonants.

For example, compare क / ख, ग / घ, त / थ, and द / ध. Practice the pairs slowly and distinctly; do not replace aspiration with extra vowel length.

## 5. Quick comparison

| Term | Core meaning |
|---|---|
| स्वर (svara) | Vowel |
| व्यञ्जन (vyañjana) | Consonant |
| अच् (ac) | Pāṇinian designation for vowels |
| हल् (hal) | Pāṇinian designation for consonants |
| उच्चारणस्थान (uccāraṇasthāna) | Place of articulation |
| घोष (ghoṣa) | Voiced |
| अघोष (aghoṣa) | Voiceless |
| अल्पप्राण (alpaprāṇa) | Unaspirated/low aspiration |
| महाप्राण (mahāprāṇa) | Aspirated/strong aspiration |

## 6. Self-assessment

### MCQs

1. Which pratyāhāra denotes the vowel class?  
   A. हल्  B. अच्  C. झष्  D. यण्  
   **Answer:** B. अच्

2. The traditional place associated with क-वर्ग is:  
   A. दन्त  B. ओष्ठ  C. कण्ठ  D. मूर्धा  
   **Answer:** C. कण्ठ

3. Which pair illustrates the traditional contrast between unaspirated and aspirated consonants?  
   A. क–ख  B. अ–आ  C. ए–ऐ  D. र–ल  
   **Answer:** A. क–ख

4. हल् is used in Pāṇinian grammar for the:  
   A. vowel class  B. consonant class  C. numbers  D. verb endings only  
   **Answer:** B. consonant class
