# Sanskrit Chapter 2 — संज्ञा एवं अव्यय

## Learning objectives

Understand the grammatical labels repeatedly used in Sanskrit texts and use common indeclinables in context.

## 1. Why संज्ञा matters

In Sanskrit grammar, a **संज्ञा** is a technical grammatical designation. Many rules become short because a single technical term represents a larger concept.

NCISM includes terms such as:

- संयोग
- संहिता
- ह्रस्व
- दीर्घ
- प्लुत
- पद
- धातु
- उपसर्ग
- गुण
- वृद्धि
- इत्
- लोप
- प्रत्याहार
- उदात्त
- अनुदात्त
- स्वरित
- सर्वण
- निपात
- प्रगृह्य

Students should know the meaning and recognise these terms while reading explanations.

## 2. संयोग

Two or more consonants occurring together without an intervening vowel are treated as a consonant cluster.

Examples:
- स्वास्थ्य
- श्लोक
- प्रज्ञा

Do not insert an unnecessary vowel while reading.

## 3. संहिता

**संहिता** broadly denotes close/continuous phonetic combination in recitation and grammar.

This becomes important when understanding why sounds change at word boundaries.

## 4. पद

A **पद** is a grammatically recognised word-form. In practical reading, distinguish the written sequence from the grammatical units hidden by sandhi.

Example:
**देवोऽपि** → देवः + अपि

The visible form and underlying forms are not always identical.

## 5. धातु

A **धातु** is a verbal root used to generate verb forms and many derived nominal forms.

Examples:
- गम् — to go
- पठ् — to read
- भू — to be/become
- कृ — to do/make
- ज्ञा — to know

Ayurvedic reading constantly depends on recognising roots behind derived words.

## 6. उपसर्ग

Common prefixes include:

**प्र, परा, अप, सम्, अनु, अव, नि, निस्/निर्, दु-/दुर्, वि, आ, अधि, अपि, अति, सु, उद्/उत्, अभि, प्रति, परि, उप**

Their effect is often to modify the meaning of a verbal root.

Example:
- गम् → प्रगमन, आगमन, निर्गमन, उपगमन

Do not assume that a prefix has only one English translation. Meaning is contextual.

## 7. गुण and वृद्धि

These are vowel-strengthening concepts used in Sanskrit grammar.

A practical introductory table:

| Basic vowel | गुण | वृद्धि |
|---|---|---|
| इ/ई | ए | ऐ |
| उ/ऊ | ओ | औ |
| ऋ/ॠ | अर् | आर् |

These rules become especially useful in sandhi and derivational grammar.

## 8. अव्यय

An **अव्यय** is an indeclinable form: it does not normally change for gender, number or case.

High-yield examples:

| अव्यय | Meaning |
|---|---|
| अत्र | here |
| तत्र | there |
| यत्र | where |
| कुतः | from where / why |
| सर्वत्र | everywhere |
| अन्यत्र | elsewhere |
| एकत्र | in one place |
| सदा | always |
| एकदा | once |
| अन्यथा | otherwise |
| किमर्थम् | for what purpose / why |
| इह | here |
| अधुना | now |
| पुनः | again |
| एव | indeed/only |
| अपि | also/even |

### Context is essential

**अपि** can mean “also”, “even”, or serve another contextual function.

Therefore: **do not translate an indeclinable mechanically without reading the whole sentence.**

## Ayurveda application

In Samhita prose, अव्यय can signal:

- time: सदा, अधुना
- place: अत्र, तत्र, सर्वत्र
- relation: यथा, तथा
- contrast: अन्यथा
- emphasis: एव
- addition: अपि

## Practice

1. Define अव्यय.
2. Give five common अव्यय with meanings.
3. Identify the उपसर्ग in उपगमन.
4. Explain why धातु recognition helps translation.
5. Distinguish गुण and वृद्धि.

### Exam tip

When asked to “construct sentences using अव्यय”, use short, grammatically safe sentences rather than complicated Sanskrit.
