# Sanskrit Chapter 9 — पर्याय, निरुक्ति एवं कोश-प्रयोग

## Learning objectives

Use Sanskrit dictionaries intelligently, identify synonyms, understand derivational explanations, and avoid false equivalence.

## 1. पर्याय

A **पर्याय** is a synonym or alternative lexical expression.

In Ayurveda, one substance or concept may have multiple names reflecting:

- morphology
- habitat
- action
- taste
- therapeutic effect
- mythological or traditional association

Therefore, synonyms are not automatically interchangeable in every context.

## 2. निरुक्ति

**निरुक्ति** examines a word through its derivational or etymological explanation.

Students should distinguish:

- **lexical meaning** — what the word means in the passage;
- **derivational meaning** — how the word is explained from a root or base.

The two may illuminate one another but are not identical.

## 3. कोश

Useful dictionary skills:

1. identify the word form;
2. determine its likely stem;
3. check gender where relevant;
4. check multiple meanings;
5. read the contextual example;
6. compare the Ayurveda usage.

## 4. Why dictionary lookup often fails

A common beginner searches:

**देहे**

and expects a dictionary entry “देहे”.

But the dictionary may list:

**देहः**

Therefore, grammatical analysis must precede lookup.

## 5. Ayurveda terminology workflow

**Text word → sandhi split → stem/root → grammar → dictionary → context → Ayurvedic meaning**

This workflow should become automatic.

## 6. Synonym table method

Maintain a personal table:

| Main term | पर्याय | Literal clue | Textual context |
|---|---|---|---|
| वायु | वात, अनिल, मारुत... | varies | physiology/pathology |
| अग्नि | पावक etc. | context dependent | digestion/metabolism |
| जल | तोय, वारि etc. | water | diet, environment, therapy |

Do not assume every poetic synonym carries the full technical definition of the main Ayurveda term.

## 7. Common error: one-word translation

A Sanskrit technical term may require a phrase in English.

For example:
**अग्नि** cannot always be translated simply as “fire”. In an Ayurvedic context its intended technical meaning may concern digestive/metabolic function.
