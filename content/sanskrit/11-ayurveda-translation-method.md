# Sanskrit Chapter 11 — Ayurveda Text Translation & Interpretation

## The real purpose of Sanskrit in BAMS

The goal is not to become a grammarian for its own sake.

The goal is to read an Ayurveda text accurately enough to understand the author's intended concept.

## 1. The seven-step method

### Step 1 — Read aloud
Read the sentence or śloka slowly and accurately.

### Step 2 — Padaccheda
Split obvious sandhi.

### Step 3 — Mark forms
For each important word, note:
- stem
- gender
- case
- number

For verbs:
- root
- person
- number
- tense/mood if recognisable

### Step 4 — Identify syntax
Mark:
- subject
- object
- instrument
- recipient
- source
- location

### Step 5 — Resolve compounds
Perform vigraha where useful.

### Step 6 — Dictionary + commentary
Use a reliable Sanskrit dictionary and then the prescribed commentary where available.

### Step 7 — Ayurveda meaning
Only now formulate the conceptual meaning.

## 2. Never translate in this order

**Sanskrit word → first English meaning → combine English words**

This often creates inaccurate translations.

Instead:

**grammar → syntax → context → concept → natural translation**

## 3. Example

**वैद्यः रोगिणं परीक्षते।**

Analysis:
- वैद्यः — nominative singular, agent
- रोगिणम् — accusative singular, patient/object
- परीक्षते — examines

Translation:
**The physician examines the patient.**

Now compare with an over-literal translation:
“The physician examines the diseased one.”

The second may be grammatically possible in some contexts but is less natural unless the context specifically means “diseased person”.

## 4. Technical terms

When a technical Ayurveda term has no exact English equivalent:

1. retain the Sanskrit term;
2. provide a concise explanatory gloss;
3. avoid forcing a modern biomedical equivalent;
4. explain the concept separately.

Example:
**दोष** → retain “doṣa” and explain its Ayurvedic conceptual role instead of automatically writing “humour”.

## 5. Commentary use

A commentary may:
- clarify grammar,
- explain difficult words,
- supply omitted context,
- resolve alternative meanings,
- connect the passage to broader Ayurveda principles.

Students should distinguish:
**मूलपाठ ≠ टीका ≠ translation**

## 6. Translation answer format

For an examination answer:

**मूलपाठ:** Sanskrit text  
**पदच्छेद:** separated words  
**अन्वय:** prose order  
**शब्दार्थ:** key word meanings  
**भावार्थ:** coherent meaning  
**आयुर्वेदिक महत्त्व:** why the statement matters

This is much stronger than a single paragraph of translation.

## Practice checklist

Before finalising a translation, ask:

- Did I split sandhi?
- Did I identify the verb?
- Did I identify case endings?
- Did I check compound words?
- Did I consult context?
- Did I preserve technical terms where necessary?
- Did I avoid adding ideas not present in the text?

## High-yield skill

If you master **padaccheda + grammar + anvaya**, your Samhita reading becomes dramatically easier.
