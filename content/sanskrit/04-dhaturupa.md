# Sanskrit Chapter 4 — धातु एवं धातुरूप

## Learning objectives

Recognise common verbal roots, understand how person/number/tense alter a verb, and use root recognition during Samhita translation.

## 1. धातु: the engine of the sentence

A Sanskrit sentence often becomes much easier when the verb is found first.

Ask:
1. What is the verb?
2. Which धातु does it come from?
3. Who performs the action?
4. In what number and tense/mood?
5. What objects or complements depend on it?

## 3. लकार: practical introduction

Students will meet labels such as:

- लट् — present
- लङ् — imperfect/past
- लृट् — future
- लोट् — imperative
- विधिलिङ् — optative/potential

The purpose at first is recognition, not memorising every Pāṇinian rule.

## 4. Present tense model: पठ्

| Person | Singular | Dual | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| प्रथम | पठति | पठतः | पठन्ति |
| मध्यम | पठसि | पठथः | पठथ |
| उत्तम | पठामि | पठावः | पठामः |

The “person” terminology is Sanskrit-specific:
- प्रथम = he/she/they
- मध्यम = you
- उत्तम = I/we

## 5. Clinical-text reading

Example:
**वैद्यः रोगिणं परीक्षते।**

- वैद्यः — subject, nominative singular
- रोगिणम् — object, accusative singular
- परीक्षते — verb, “examines”

Natural translation:
**The physician examines the patient.**

## 6. आत्मनेपद and परस्मैपद

Some verbs take forms associated with **परस्मैपद**, others with **आत्मनेपद**, and some are उभयपदी.

At first, learn each high-frequency verb together with its attested form rather than trying to infer every form from English.

## 7. Why धातु knowledge matters in Ayurveda

Many familiar Ayurveda terms are built from verbal roots:

- चिकित्सा
- सेवन
- पाचन
- शोधन
- वमन
- विरेचन
- ग्रहण
- धारण
- निदान

Root recognition helps you move from a dictionary gloss to a meaningful contextual interpretation.

## Practice

1. What is a धातु?
2. Identify the root in पठन.
3. Translate पठति.
4. Identify subject and verb in “वैद्यः रोगिणं परीक्षते”.
5. Name three common लकार.

### Exam tip

If you do not recognise a verb, **do not guess its translation from its first few letters**. Find the stem/root and use context.
